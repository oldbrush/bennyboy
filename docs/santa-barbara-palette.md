# Santa Barbara Color Palette

> Based on the City of Santa Barbara's enforced Mediterranean/Spanish Colonial Revival aesthetic — a palette designed for visual cohesion and harmony with the coastal landscape.
>
> Source: [santabarbaraca.gov](https://santabarbaraca.gov)

---

## Palette Philosophy

Santa Barbara's architectural review codes mandate a traditional Mediterranean vocabulary. The palette draws from **adobe stucco, terracotta clay, wrought iron, and coastal foliage** — materials that have defined the American Riviera since the 1925 rebuild. Color serves form: warm, sun-bleached surfaces punctuated by rich earth accents, with restraint favored over boldness.

## Application Rationale

The palette preserves the site's dark luxury foundation while replacing cool-neutral tones with the warm, sun-baked character mandated by Santa Barbara's architectural codes:

- **Teal → Terracotta**: The primary accent shifts from a generic teal to terracotta, directly referencing the city's iconic red clay rooflines.
- **Cool charcoal → Warm charcoal**: Background tones gain brown/amber undertones, grounding the palette in earth rather than steel.
- **Cream text warms up**: Text colors shift from neutral cream to sandy warm-white, matching the stucco vocabulary.
- **Sage green accents**: Subtle green touches echo the coastal sage and olive trees that frame Santa Barbara's estates.

This approach maintains the sophisticated, editorial feel of the original design while making the site feel unmistakably *of* Santa Barbara.
